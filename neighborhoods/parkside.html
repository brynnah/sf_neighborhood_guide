Parkside

General Overview

Parkside became an official neighborhood in 1905, when a syndicate led by William Crocker bought land from Adolph Sutro’s estate, and from others, for a large development. The name comes from a “park” that was then just the trees around what is now Pine Lake. The area was remote and mostly sand dunes. The first homes were built in groups of four near Stern Grove, for working families. Early residents have been described as pioneers in the wilderness, because there were few roads or lights to find the way home after the streetcar.

Residents organized. The Parkside District Improvement Club lobbied for streetcar and sewer lines, parks, schools, libraries, and a fire station, and it hosted festivals, dinners, balls, and children’s fairs. Parkside today is mostly houses on the avenue grid, south of the Sunset, with Taraval Street as the commercial and transit corridor.

Source: Western Neighborhoods Project

Food History

Parkside’s food history is tied to early 20th-century neighborhood groceries, family-run butcher shops, and American and Asian diners. A newer stretch of the neighborhood is known for Northern Chinese dumpling houses, including Kingdom of Dumpling, Dumpling Specialist, and Dumpling Kitchen.

Important Locations

  • Taraval Street, a commercial corridor with mid-20th-century neighborhood architecture, including 1920s period-revival Mediterranean storefronts.
  • Stern Grove, a park given to the city in 1931 by Rosalie Stern in memory of her husband.
  • Pine Lake Park, the grove and lake the neighborhood was named around.
