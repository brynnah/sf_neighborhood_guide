Presidio

General Overview

Before Spanish colonization, the land around the Golden Gate was home to the Ramaytush Ohlone. In 1776 Spain built a military fort, a presidio, overlooking San Francisco Bay. It remained a military post under Mexico from 1822 to 1846. The United States Army took it over after the Mexican-American War and used it through the Civil War, the world wars, and the Cold War. Soldiers from those wars are buried at San Francisco National Cemetery inside the Presidio.

The land was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1962. In 1972 Congress created the Golden Gate National Recreation Area and said the Presidio would join it if the Army left. The Department of Defense announced base closures in 1989, and the question of how to turn the post into a park led Representative Nancy Pelosi to create the Presidio Trust, a federal agency to manage the site with the National Park Service. To avoid raising taxes, the Trust funds itself by leasing former military buildings. The Presidio Trust Act passed in 1996, and the Trust began operating in 1998. The Presidio now has about 3,000 residents and 200 organizations, along with threatened plants and animals. Crissy Field, the Main Post, and the trails toward the Golden Gate Bridge are the parts most people visit.

Source: Presidio Trust

Food History

As a Spanish and Mexican military post, the Presidio’s food was basic rations for a garrison, mixed with regional Spanish and Mexican cooking and farming around the bay. Under the U.S. Army, eating shifted to communal mess halls. After the post became a park, the food changed again. Tunnel Tops brought cafes and a food hall, and restaurants include dining that refers back to the Presidio’s Spanish and Mexican past. Arguello, at the Presidio Officers’ Club, is in a building that was a mess hall during the Army years.

Sources: Presidio dining, Presidio of San Francisco

Important Locations

  • Presidio Officers’ Club, now home to Arguello, in a former Army mess hall.
  • Fort Point, the brick fort under the Golden Gate Bridge.
  • Crissy Field and the bayfront path.
  • The Main Post and the historic military buildings.
  • San Francisco National Cemetery.
  • Golden Gate Bridge approaches and trails.
