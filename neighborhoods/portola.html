Portola

General Overview

The Portola is a historic working-class neighborhood in southeastern San Francisco, between Bernal Heights, the Bayview, and John McLaren Park. For thousands of years, the Ohlone lived in villages in Visitacion Valley and along Mission Creek. Spanish colonization in the 18th century displaced them. Ranchos, then homesteaders, divided the land into small farms, greenhouses, and businesses.

While the Portola was still rural at the edge of the city, it was known as San Francisco’s Garden District because flowers and produce for the city were grown there. Over the last century it has been home to many immigrant communities. San Bruno Avenue is the local commercial street, and the hillside blocks are mostly houses.

Source: Portola Neighborhood Association

Food History

The Portola grew from an agricultural truck-garden and floral district into a multi-ethnic working-class neighborhood. It is known for longtime family-run businesses and the food scene along San Bruno Avenue.

Source: Kneedeep Times

Important Locations

  • San Bruno Avenue, the neighborhood’s commercial and food street.
  • John McLaren Park, along the southern and western edge.
  • Burrows Pocket Park.
  • San Francisco Music Hall of Fame.
  • The site of the neighborhood’s historic greenhouses, from its years as a garden district.
