Cow Hollow

General Overview

In 1776, San Francisco’s first settlement was at the Presidio, and the land that became Cow Hollow was springs, meadows, and sand hills. In the mid-1800s, settlers farmed around a lagoon in an area then called Spring Valley. Dairy farming followed and gave the neighborhood its name. Union Street linked the Presidio to the growing city during the Gold Rush, and Victorian mansions went up in the 1860s and 1870s. In 1891 the city removed the livestock as the area developed and filled the lagoon to make room for more houses.

Cow Hollow is now a residential neighborhood of Victorian and Edwardian houses, many of which survived the 1906 earthquake and fire. Union Street is the shopping row of boutiques and restaurants between the Marina and Pacific Heights.

Source: Union Street landmarks

Food History

Cow Hollow was once San Francisco’s largest agricultural area. The dairy herds numbered in the hundreds, and Chinese immigrants kept large vegetable gardens, selling produce on the street and to local cooks. By the late 19th century, tanneries, slaughterhouses, and sausage factories had moved in. Those industries were later cleared as the neighborhood became residential.

Union Street is now known for boutiques and fine dining. Wildseed, at 2000 Union Street, is a fully vegan restaurant.

Important Locations

  • McElroy Octagon House (2645 Gough Street), built in 1861, is one of two octagon houses left in San Francisco. It is now a decorative arts museum and garden run by the National Society of the Colonial Dames of America, and it is open to the public.
  • Casebolt House (2727 Pierce Street) is an Italianate Victorian from about 1865, recognized as the oldest surviving house in Cow Hollow (San Francisco Landmark No. 51).
  • Cudworth Mansion and the Twin Wedding Houses (2040–2044 Union Street) were built in the 1870s by dairy rancher James Cudworth. The property includes a historic farmhouse and barn that served as a first-aid station in 1906. Cudworth built two matching Victorian houses as wedding presents for his daughters. They now hold Union Street shops and offices.
  • The Sherman House (2160 Green Street) is a historic residence in the neighborhood.
  • Union Street is the neighborhood’s shopping and restaurant row.
