Nob Hill / Russian Hill

General Overview

Nob Hill is one of San Francisco’s wealthiest neighborhoods. In the Gilded Age, Leland Stanford and other railroad tycoons lived here. Four of the men who financed the transcontinental railroad kept mansions on the hill. Most of that housing was destroyed in the 1906 earthquake. The mansions were not rebuilt. Hotels went up in their place, including the grand hotels still on the hill. Grace Cathedral and Huntington Park sit at the top, and cable cars climb the slope.

Today Nob Hill’s residents are split fairly evenly between Asian and white, about 40 percent each, and a large share of the Asian population is Chinese. Russian Hill is just north of Nob Hill, known for the crooked block of Lombard Street and the Hyde Street cable car. Both hills are dense with apartments and houses and look out over the bay.

Source: Nob Hill

Food History

Nob Hill’s restaurants follow its money: a lot of the dining is expensive. A few places are more everyday. The Thonglor, at 420 Geary Street, serves Thai food. Bob’s Donuts, at 1720 Polk Street, is a longtime city donut shop. Mini Potstickers, at 1639 Polk Street, is the second location of a Chinese restaurant whose original is in the Sunset.

Source: The Infatuation

Important Locations

  • Huntington Park, a historic park at the top of Nob Hill.
  • Grace Cathedral, beside the park.
  • InterContinental Mark Hopkins, on the site of Mark Hopkins’s Gilded Age mansion. Its Big 4 restaurant is named for the four railroad barons of Nob Hill.
  • Lombard Street, the crooked block on Russian Hill.
  • The cable car lines on Powell and Hyde streets.
