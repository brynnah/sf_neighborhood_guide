Fisherman's Wharf

General Overview

Fisherman’s Wharf took shape around 1900, after the Gold Rush had brought Italian and Chinese immigrants to California. Italian fishermen introduced lateen-rigged wooden sailboats called feluccas. As Bay shipping grew, it took over the original docks where Vallejo and Union streets met the water, and the fishing fleet moved to the present wharf.

Fishermen sold part of the catch to customers right off the boats. Some set up stalls on the piers and made a market. Today’s wharf sits on the rubble of buildings destroyed in the 1906 earthquake. It is still a working harbor as well as a visitor waterfront along Jefferson Street, facing Alcatraz, with North Beach and Russian Hill just inland.

Sources: Fisherman’s Wharf, FoundSF

Food History

In 1916, fisherman Tom Castagnola opened a restaurant and expanded the idea of selling clam chowder from the boats. He is credited with the Dungeness crab cocktail, which helped set the seafood cooking the wharf is known for. Other fishermen followed him, leaving the boats to open food businesses and buying the catch from those who kept fishing. Fishing and food are still the wharf’s main attractions.

Source: Inkandescent on Castagnola’s

Important Locations

  • Pier 39, including the sea lion viewing area.
  • The Fisherman’s Wharf sign, marking the entrance to the historic fish-market docks.
  • Musée Mécanique, one of the world’s largest privately owned collections of vintage coin-operated mechanical musical instruments and arcade games.
  • USS Pampanito, a restored World War II Balao-class submarine and National Historic Landmark.
  • Ghirardelli Square, the former Ghirardelli chocolate factory, now shops, dining, and chocolate and ice cream counters.
  • Hyde Street Pier and Jefferson Street, the working harbor and the main visitor walk.
